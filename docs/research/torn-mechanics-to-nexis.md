# Torn Game-Design Patterns → Nexis Equivalents

**Purpose.** This document extracts general system-design *patterns* from Torn (torn.com), a long-running browser RPG, and proposes original, Nexis-flavored equivalents. It does not copy Torn item names, faction names, location names, flavor text, art, or exact numeric formulas — every name in the "Nexis equivalent" column below is invented for this document and has no connection to Torn's content. Numbers cited from Torn (percentages, durations, costs) describe *structure* (e.g. "cost scales with tier," "bonus stacks across sources up to a cap") and are not meant to be copied verbatim into Nexis's balance sheet.

**Grounding.** Recommendations build on what Nexis already has, confirmed by reading the live source in this worktree:
- **Education** (`server/data/educationData.js`) — foundation + specialist branches with backend-enforced `prerequisites`, `durationDays`, `costGold`, `workingStatRewards`, `unlocksSystems`.
- **Travel** (`server/data/travelData.js`, `server/services/organizationService.js`) — server-authoritative routes with `danger`/`durationMs`, transport tiers, cargo, escorts, and a `World Geography` course gate on travel-discovery events. The Guild member roster already surfaces a live `Travelling: <destination>` location string per member.
- **Consortiums** (`server/data/consortiumTypes.js`) — 10 types, star-tier reward trees, and an existing `CONSORTIUM_RANDOM_REWARD_POOLS` per-type random-reward system; founding gated by a `Civic Fundamentals` course.
- **Guilds** (`server/services/organizationService.js`) — real backend with roles/members/logs, a `GUILD_SKILL_TREE` of 6 fixed nodes across 4 tiers, armory deposit/withdraw, 9 `GUILD_QUESTS` templates, and 3 `GUILD_DUNGEONS`. **No war/territory/PvP mechanic exists** — confirmed gap.
- **Legacy Points** (`server/data/legacyAchievementsData.js`) — 55 achievements split into `honor`/`medal` kinds across 11 categories, each granting `rewardPoints`, already modeled on Torn's 1-point-per-achievement pattern.
- **Home page** (`src/pages/Home.tsx`) — already a "compact command center": hero identity/status line, Current Activity, Quick Actions, Progression Snapshot, and a right rail of Notifications, Property/Inventory/Equipment *summary* panels that link out to full pages.

Where Nexis already implements a pattern, the table says so and marks priority low / approval "no" (parity confirmation, not new work). Where it's a genuine gap, priority and approval reflect that.

## 1. Education

| Torn feature | Why it works | Nexis equivalent (original name) | Implementation priority | Risk/abuse concern | Needs user approval? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prerequisite-gated subject trees (foundation courses unlock specialist courses) | Creates a visible progression ladder and prevents players from rushing high-value courses before they're "ready" | Already implemented as Foundation/Specialist branches with `prerequisites` in `educationData.js` | low | none — parity only | no |
| A capstone "final course" per subject that requires every other course in that subject first, and always grants a unique unlock | Gives long-horizon players a clear, prestigious finish line per tree, distinct from the incremental courses along the way | **Mastery Diplomas** — one capstone course per specialist branch, gated on completing every other course in that branch, granting a unique location/system unlock not available any other way | medium | could make one branch feel mandatory if its unlock is too strong; needs careful reward parity across branches | yes |
| Education-time reduction stacking from multiple independent sources (job-rank passive, an investment-holding passive, spent currency), combined but soft-capped rather than unlimited | Rewards long-term investment across systems without letting min-maxers reduce course time to near-zero | **Study Efficiency** — stacking reduction from a Civic job-rank passive, a Consortium bond-holding passive, and Legacy Point spend, combined under an explicit soft cap | medium | uncapped stacking would trivialize course duration, undermining the time-gate that makes courses meaningful | yes |
| Courses tied to running a business also boost that business's performance (a "marketing" course improves company advertising) | Cross-links two systems so investing in one payoff-generates value in the other, encouraging players to engage with both | Specialist courses that boost Consortium advertising/output growth, extending the existing `Civic Fundamentals` → Consortium-founding link | medium | if too strong, could make Consortium success purely education-gated rather than gameplay-gated | yes |
| Capstone course completions unlock new map locations or vendor tiers, not just stat bonuses | Turns education into a form of exploration progression, not just a stat treadmill | **District Access** — extend the pattern already used by `World Geography` (which unlocks travel-discovery events) to other specialist capstones, each unlocking a distinct map location or vendor tier | medium | needs enough locations in the content pipeline to back every branch, or some branches feel worse than others | yes |
| A passive bonus earned from reaching the top rank of an early "starter" job, kept permanently even after leaving that job | Rewards early-game engagement with a permanent account upgrade, softening the loss of switching careers later | **Apprenticeship Legacy** — a permanent passive (e.g. education-speed or working-stat bonus) earned by maxing a starter Civic job track, retained after moving to other work | medium | needs to be small enough that it doesn't dominate the choice of which starter job to run | yes |
| Awards/achievements tied specifically to education milestones (starting first course, finishing a subject, finishing a capstone) | Gives the achievement system content that reinforces the education loop instead of sitting parallel to it | Extend the existing Legacy Points `Education` category with entries for each Mastery Diploma and for full-branch completion | low | grants new Legacy Points, so it's a (small) new reward source | yes |
| Interrupting/restarting a course as a consequence of certain risky player behavior during study | Adds light stakes to an otherwise passive system and discourages doing something reckless mid-course | **Course Forfeiture** — restart-from-zero penalty triggered by a defined risky action taken while enrolled (e.g. getting jailed/hospitalized via reckless play) | low | can feel punitive/unfair if the triggering condition isn't crystal clear to the player; needs strong warning UI before it ships | yes |

## 2. Travel

| Torn feature | Why it works | Nexis equivalent (original name) | Implementation priority | Risk/abuse concern | Needs user approval? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A persistent "in transit" status shown on the player's public profile/roster entry, including destination | Lets other players (allies and rivals alike) understand at a glance why someone isn't responding, and creates soft tension around being caught away from home | Already partially implemented — Guild member view shows `Travelling: <destination>` (`organizationService.js`). Extend the same string to the public profile page | low | none for the base case | no |
| An option for certain roles/professions to hide their travel destination from others | Gives stealth-oriented playstyles a mechanical reason to exist and protects players from being tracked | **Discreet Passage** — a perk (from a specific Civic job, item, or Guild skill) that masks destination on the public status line while still showing "traveling" | low-medium | could be used to dodge accountability in guild logistics if not scoped carefully | yes |
| Full immunity while physically in transit, plus a short vulnerability grace window right after arriving | Removes "gotcha" ambush risk from an activity that's otherwise unavoidable, while still preserving some post-arrival tension | **Transit Ward** (in-transit safety) + **Landing Grace** (short post-arrival window before any threat/event can target the player) | medium | mainly relevant once Nexis has PvP/ambush mechanics (see Guilds §4); low urgency until then, but useful pattern to reserve now | yes |
| Carry-capacity increased by a property upgrade + hired specialist, a purchasable capacity item, and a faction-granted bonus — all stacking | Turns "how much can I bring home" into a long-term investment target across multiple systems, driving demand for property, hired NPCs, items, and guild membership alike | Extend existing cargo/transport tiers with a purchasable **Caravan Trunk** capacity item and a Guild-skill capacity bonus (a natural extension of the existing `Quartermaster Cache` node) | medium | stacking capacity bonuses too generously could flood city markets and crash prices on imported goods | yes |
| Destination shops split into distinct categories (general goods / weapons & armor / restricted-goods) that vary by location | Makes each destination feel distinct and gives players a reason to visit specific places rather than always going wherever is fastest | Formalize a three-tier vendor taxonomy per city (general / equipment / restricted) consistent with each city's `danger`/`role` fields already in `cityData.js` | medium | restricted-goods tier needs clear rules so it doesn't become a de facto black market bypassing existing economy controls | yes |
| Travel time reduced by a property upgrade, a hired specialist, and a purchasable higher travel-class ticket, all independently | Gives players several different investment paths to the same payoff (faster travel), suited to different playstyles/budgets | Extend existing transport tiers with a purchasable **Swift Passage** upgrade and confirm the property/specialist reduction paths already implied by "transport tiers, cargo, escorts" | medium | needs a floor on minimum travel time so danger/duration balancing on each route still matters | yes |
| Bringing goods bought abroad back home to sell into the local market, creating price-arbitrage-driven trade play | This is one of Torn's strongest emergent-economy engines — it turns travel from a stat-grind into an active trading minigame with real player-driven price discovery | **Caravan Trade Runs** — formalize cross-city price differentials on tradeable goods so hauling cargo between cities is reliably profitable, building directly on the existing route/cargo system | high | primary lever for gold-farming bots/multi-accounting; needs cargo caps, escort risk, and possibly a diminishing-returns curve on repeated identical runs | yes |
| Travel gated behind a level (or equivalent) threshold | Keeps new players from wandering into high-danger routes before they have the stats/items to survive them | Confirm/audit existing level + `World Geography` gate combination for parity with this pattern | low | none — parity check | no |
| Most non-travel actions locked out while abroad or in transit | Reinforces that travel is a real commitment, not a costless toggle, and gives danger/duration numbers actual weight | Confirm/audit existing server-authoritative action-lock behavior while traveling | low | none — parity check | no |
| Achievement tied to cumulative time/distance spent traveling | Rewards a playstyle (frequent traveling/trading) that doesn't otherwise generate obvious achievement progress | Extend the existing Legacy Points `Travel` category with cumulative-time and cumulative-route entries | low | grants new Legacy Points, small new reward source | yes |

## 3. Companies (Consortiums)

| Torn feature | Why it works | Nexis equivalent (original name) | Implementation priority | Risk/abuse concern | Needs user approval? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A three-metric performance model (staff-fit/activity efficiency, workspace-crowding environment, income-relative-to-peers standing) that together determine overall rank | Gives a director several distinct levers to manage instead of one flat "score," and makes running a company feel like active management rather than a passive number | **Workshop Performance** — a three-metric model (Efficiency / Conditions / Standing) layered onto the existing Consortium star-tier system | high | if any one metric dominates the others, directors will optimize only that one and ignore real management; needs balanced weighting | yes |
| Director receives a small daily passive working-stat trickle, scaling with the company's star rank | Rewards the director role specifically (distinct from regular employees) for successfully growing the business over time | **Director's Draw** — passive working-stat gain to the Consortium's founder/director, scaling with star tier | medium | needs to stay modest relative to normal stat-gain methods so it doesn't become the optimal grind path | yes |
| Director receives extra free training uses per day, scaling with star rank | A second, separate reward channel for successful management, on top of the passive stat trickle | **Foreman's Training Slate** — bonus stat-training charges per day for the director, scaling with star tier | medium | same as above — must stay proportionate | yes |
| A currency earned from company rank (points-per-star, granted periodically) that's spent on unlockable "specials" — either active abilities or standing passive perks | Gives directors an explicit, spendable reward for growing their company, separate from raw gold income | Confirm whether the existing `CONSORTIUM_RANDOM_REWARD_POOLS`/star-tier reward trees already cover this, or add an explicit **Foreman Points** currency if the reward trees are purely automatic/random rather than player-chosen | medium | if it becomes a second gold-equivalent currency, it needs its own sinks or it inflates the broader economy | yes |
| Random bonus-reward pool per company type, delivered at certain performance thresholds | Adds variance and surprise to an otherwise deterministic progression, and reinforces each company type's flavor via type-specific rewards | Already implemented — `CONSORTIUM_RANDOM_REWARD_POOLS` in `consortiumTypes.js` covers this per type | low | none — parity only | no |
| Structured employee application flow: candidates can apply to several companies at once, applications persist for a few days and can be withdrawn/edited, director accepts/declines | Removes the "apply and wait, apply and wait" friction of a one-at-a-time system while still keeping hiring an active director decision | **Consortium Roster Requests** — multi-apply, time-limited, director-reviewed hiring flow; addresses what looks like a current gap, since Consortiums are described only via types/star-trees/founding gate, not an employee/position system | high | this is a foundational gap for making Consortiums feel like real player-run businesses rather than static reward trees | yes |
| New hires are restricted from certain special-ability access and from being force-trained by the director for a short probation window | Prevents a "hire, extract value, immediately fire" abuse loop and gives both sides a trial period | **Onboarding Hold** — short probation window on new Consortium roster members before they can access special-tier rewards or be reassigned | medium | directly closes an abuse vector, so worth pairing with the roster-requests feature above rather than shipping standalone | yes |
| Director-controlled daily advertising/outreach spend that's more effective when paired with specific education courses or a dedicated "marketer" role | Ties gold spend, a role assignment, and the education system together into one growth lever, reinforcing cross-system investment | **Outreach Budget** — director-set daily spend on Consortium growth, boosted by specific specialist education courses (see Education §1, row 3) and/or a dedicated roster role | high | needs diminishing returns on spend to avoid pure pay-to-win scaling for whichever consortium has the deepest gold reserves | yes |

## 4. Factions (Guilds)

| Torn feature | Why it works | Nexis equivalent (original name) | Implementation priority | Risk/abuse concern | Needs user approval? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New members enter on a default, permission-limited rank for a short fixed window before promotion is possible | Protects guild resources (armory, treasury) from being drained immediately by a bad-faith or compromised new join | Confirm/extend Guild roles with an explicit probation rank (**Initiate**) with a fixed permission-limited window, if not already enforced server-side | medium | this is itself a risk-mitigation feature — the gap (if it exists) is the risk | yes |
| Multi-apply, time-limited, editable/withdrawable guild-join applications | Reduces new-player friction finding a home guild without spamming leaders with unmanaged noise | **Guild Charter Requests** — multi-apply flow with a fixed expiry, building on the existing roles/members backend | medium | none significant — mostly a UX/backend flow improvement | yes |
| Shared armory items can either be permanently donated (one-way) or loaned (recallable, tracked to the lender) | Gives members a way to share gear without fully giving it up, increasing willingness to contribute to the shared pool | Add a recallable **Guild Loan** mode alongside the existing armory deposit/withdraw, distinct from an outright donation | medium | loan-recall could be used to yank gear mid-use as a griefing move against a guildmate; needs a recall cooldown or notice period | yes |
| Leaders/co-leaders can create custom named ranks with fine-grained, individually toggled permissions (armory access, quest/operation management, etc.) | Lets each guild organize itself the way it wants instead of being boxed into a fixed rank list, which matters a lot once guilds grow past a handful of members | **Guild Charters** — a custom-position editor for Guild leadership, if the current role system is fixed rather than leader-defined | high | permission mistakes (over-granting armory/treasury access) are the leading cause of internal guild theft in games like this; needs a clear warning UI and an audit log (which Nexis already has via guild logs) | yes |
| Group operations gated by a shared, regenerating "capacity" resource so a guild can't run unlimited operations simultaneously regardless of size | Prevents large guilds from simply brute-forcing every available operation at once, keeping smaller/mid-size guilds competitive | **Operation Capacity** — a regenerating resource that caps how many `GUILD_QUESTS`/`GUILD_DUNGEONS` a guild can have active at once, independent of member count | medium | without this, guild size becomes a pure power multiplier with no diminishing returns | yes |
| Multi-stage operation chains where only the final stage in the chain pays out, raising both risk and required coordination | Creates a distinct "high commitment, high payout" operation type layered on top of the normal one-and-done operations | **Linked Contracts** — chain 2-3 existing `GUILD_QUESTS` templates so only completing the full sequence releases the (larger) reward | medium | if a chain fails partway, contributed time/resources are lost; needs very clear UI framing so it doesn't feel like a trap | yes |
| **No war/territory/PvP system today — this is the single largest structural gap identified.** Torn's version: reputation earned from combat against rival factions, passive reputation from held territory, a timed "capture the objective" war structure, and a distinct zero-sum raid variant | This is the core reason factions matter competitively in Torn — it converts faction membership from "a shared quest/armory club" into an ongoing rivalry system with real stakes, which is a major driver of long-term retention | **Guild Warfare** — a new foundational system: territory holdings granting passive Reputation, a timed wall/objective-capture war mode, and a separate zero-sum raid mode. This is the highest-leverage single addition in this whole document given it is an explicitly confirmed gap | high | this is a major new PvP surface: needs matchmaking/size-parity safeguards so small guilds can't be endlessly farmed by large ones, clear opt-in/inactive-guild protections, and careful economy tuning since it's also a new Reputation-equivalent lever | yes |
| A consecutive-attack "streak" counter with milestone flat bonuses and a slowly-scaling multiplier that discourages both giving up early and expecting runaway late-streak power | Rewards sustained, coordinated group activity without letting the reward curve spiral out of control | **Warpath Streak** — part of the Guild Warfare system above; a streak counter with fixed milestone rewards and a deliberately slow-growing multiplier curve (not unlimited) | high | streak systems are a classic target for bot/multi-account farming; needs the same damping curve shape Torn uses (slow multiplier growth, not linear) plus cooldowns | yes |
| A skill tree split into a small fixed "core" branch everyone gets, plus a larger pool of optional specialization branches where a guild picks a limited number active at once (with the ability to respec/swap) | Gives guild leadership a real build-identity decision instead of a single linear unlock path, and keeps the system replayable as guild strategy shifts | Expand the existing 6-node, single-path `GUILD_SKILL_TREE` into a fixed core branch plus a larger pool of optional specialization branches with a capped number active at once — this directly addresses the "under-utilized" skill tree flagged as a known gap | high | respec/swap costs need tuning so guilds can adapt strategy without making the choice meaningless by being freely reversible every day | yes |
| Leadership can view and adjust individual members' contributed money/points balances, gated by permission | Gives leadership the tools to manage shared resources fairly and transparently, backed by an audit trail | Confirm/extend the existing Guild treasury/log system to expose per-member contribution balances to permitted roles | medium | balance-adjustment power is a significant trust surface; must be tied to the guild's existing logs for accountability | yes |

## 5. Achievements / Awards / Merits (Legacy Points)

| Torn feature | Why it works | Nexis equivalent (original name) | Implementation priority | Risk/abuse concern | Needs user approval? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two-tier award taxonomy: one-time "did the thing" recognitions vs. progressive/stacking recognitions with escalating thresholds | Lets the achievement list cover both quick early wins and long-term grinds without needing two separate systems | Already implemented — `LEGACY_ACHIEVEMENT_KINDS` (`honor`/`medal`) in `legacyAchievementsData.js` | low | none — parity only | no |
| Achievement categories spanning every major game system (combat, crime, travel, education, company/guild, misc/time) | Makes the achievement list a reflection of the whole game rather than one narrow slice of it, and gives every playstyle a path to earn points | Already implemented — 11 categories in `LEGACY_ACHIEVEMENT_CATEGORIES`; extend with entries as Guild Warfare, Consortium rosters, and Mastery Diplomas ship (see §1–§4) | low | new categories/entries are a small new reward source each time they're added | yes |
| A single earned-point currency spendable on permanent account upgrades, where each upgrade path has an escalating per-level cost (level 1 cheap, each subsequent level costs one more point than the last, capped at a fixed max level) | This is the core sink that gives achievement-hunting a long-term purpose beyond bragging rights, and the escalating cost naturally throttles how fast anyone can max a given path | Already the explicit design basis for Legacy Points per the existing 55-achievement system — audit current spend-sink balance for parity with this escalating-cost pattern rather than building something new | low | audit/tuning task on an already-approved system, not a new mechanic | no |
| Secondary passive income into the same point currency (e.g., a small grant every few levels), plus an optional way to convert a separate currency into points | Keeps the point economy from feeling entirely achievement-gated, giving grindier or buyable alternate paths to the same permanent upgrades | **Legacy Milestone Trickle** — small Legacy Point grant every N character levels, as an income stream independent of the achievement list | medium | a second income stream changes the economy's overall point supply; needs to be modeled against the existing achievement-point supply, not layered on carelessly | yes |
| Bonus stacking across multiple sources is combined but explicitly capped, not simply additive without limit | Prevents min-maxers from combining every available bonus source into a degenerate stack that breaks balance elsewhere | Formal stacking-cap governance rule applied to any future Legacy Point-funded bonus (ties into the Study Efficiency cap proposed in §1) | medium | this is itself the risk-mitigation; the risk is *not* having it once multiple bonus sources exist | yes |
| Achievement browsing split into a categorized, progress-tracked view and a flat searchable full list | Serves two different player needs — "what should I work on next" (categorized) and "did I already get X" (searchable) — without cluttering either view | Pure UI enhancement to the existing Legacy Points page: categorized/progress view plus a flat searchable list | low | none — presentation only, no new mechanic or numbers | no |
| An extra bonus for completing every achievement within a whole category, on top of the per-achievement points already earned | Gives completionist players a clear "collect them all" goal per category, adding a light layer of prestige on top of the base grind | **Chronicle Completion Bonus** — extra Legacy Points awarded for 100%-clearing a category | medium | small new reward, but needs its value tuned so it doesn't dwarf the value of the individual achievements it sits on top of | yes |

## 6. Home Page / UI Density

| Torn feature | Why it works | Nexis equivalent (original name) | Implementation priority | Risk/abuse concern | Needs user approval? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Persistent "vitals" bars for core resources, each with a live countdown timer and a click-through straight to the relevant action page | Keeps the single most important "can I act right now, and on what" information visible at all times without navigating anywhere, while doubling as a navigation shortcut | Nexis's Home already surfaces most of this via the Current Activity / Progression Snapshot panels as static rows; convert the resource-bar-shaped rows into live progress bars with timers and click-through, matching what's already partly there | low | none — presentation only, no new numbers or mechanics | no |
| A single, always-visible status line describing the player's current state (free to act / traveling-with-destination / incapacitated / etc.) | One line answers "what is this player doing right now" for both the player themselves and, on a profile, anyone looking at them — high information density for very little screen space | Nexis's `Home.tsx` hero already shows a condition label and city name; confirm it also reflects live travel destination (not just city), matching the `Travelling: <destination>` pattern already used in the Guild roster view | low | none — mostly already at parity, minor extension | no |
| Notification/message icons with unread counts, persistently visible near the top of the page regardless of scroll position | Ensures players never miss something actionable without needing a full page dedicated to it | Nexis's Home already has a Notifications/Readiness panel as a list; consider promoting an unread-count badge into the persistent header alongside it | low | none — presentation only | no |
| Deep functionality (full company management, full faction management, full market) deliberately kept off the homepage entirely — home only shows a compact summary plus a link out to the dedicated page | This is the core discipline that keeps a homepage information-dense without becoming overwhelming: summarize state, don't replicate full UI | Nexis already follows this exactly via the Property/Inventory/Equipment "Summary" panels that link to `/housing` and `/inventory`; apply the identical pattern to any new panels from Guild Warfare or Consortium rosters when those ship (§3–§4) | low | none — this is a design principle to keep following, not new work | no |
| Every summary row that has an obvious destination page is itself a clickable link, not just the panels with explicit "Manage" rows | Removes friction — a player scanning a status row shouldn't have to hunt for a separate link elsewhere on the panel to act on what they just read | Audit `Home.tsx` for any static (non-linked) rows — e.g. Current Education, Travel status — that describe something with an obvious destination page, and convert them to inline links, matching the pattern the Property/Inventory panels already use | low | none — presentation only, no new mechanic | no |
| A compact grid of one-click shortcuts to the most common daily actions, positioned prominently on the homepage | Reduces the number of clicks for the actions a player takes every single session, which matters disproportionately for daily-engagement retention | Already implemented via the `quickActions`/`home-actions-grid` in `Home.tsx` | low | none — parity only | no |

### Summary of highest-leverage gaps

Two items stand out as genuinely new mechanics rather than parity/polish work: **Guild Warfare** (§4) is the single biggest structural gap — Nexis Guilds have no war/territory/PvP system at all today, and Torn's territory-plus-raid-plus-chaining structure is the primary reason factions matter competitively there. **Consortium Roster Requests + Outreach Budget** (§3) is the second — Consortiums currently read as star-tier reward trees without a real employee/hiring loop, and Torn's advertising-boosted-by-education pattern would also strengthen the existing Education-to-Consortium link via `Civic Fundamentals`. Both require full design/approval before any implementation work begins.
