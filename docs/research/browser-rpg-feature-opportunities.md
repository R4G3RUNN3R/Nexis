# Browser RPG Feature Opportunities

Research pass across persistent browser/text-based RPGs to surface mechanics that
could inform Nexis's roadmap. This covers the crime-empire/mafia genre, the
Cyber Nations-style nation-management genre, Kingdom of Loathing's zone/item-gate
progression, Melvor Idle's skill-interdependency patterns (filtered heavily —
Nexis is not going idle), and a handful of other notable persistent text/browser
games with real player economies or faction systems (Legend of the Green Dragon,
Fallen London/StoryNexus, NationStates, Urban Dead, Dark Throne). Torn is included
only briefly/comparatively, since a separate research thread is covering Torn in
depth.

**Methodology note:** findings below are paraphrased from public wikis, official
sites, and secondary coverage (linked implicitly by game name; no proper nouns,
item names, exact formulas, or flavor text are copied verbatim). Every "how to
adapt" suggestion invents an original Nexis-flavored name rather than reusing the
source game's terminology. Where a game's core loop doesn't fit a Torn-style
dense-UI persistent browser RPG (nation simulation, full idle/offline play), that
is called out explicitly in "What to avoid" and the row is marked low priority.
Everything in this document is a new idea for discussion — nothing here is
pre-approved for implementation.

| Source/game | Mechanic observed | Why it may help Nexis | How to adapt it into Nexis identity (original naming) | What to avoid | Priority | User approval required before implementation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Torn (brief, comparative — covered in depth by a separate research thread) | Travel to a destination puts the character in a locked, timed state with reduced functionality until arrival; housing/vehicle upgrades can shorten the timer. | Nexis already has a server-authoritative Travel system, so this is confirmation rather than a new idea — the "locked state with a few upgrade levers to shorten it" pattern is proven at scale. | No new mechanic proposed here; flagged only so implementation doesn't duplicate work already scoped in the sibling Torn research doc. | Don't build a second, competing travel-timer design in parallel with the Torn-focused research thread — reconcile before touching Travel code. | low | yes |
| Torn (brief, comparative) | A single broad point currency earned from level-ups/milestones is spent on many small percentage-based permanent upgrades spanning combat, education, property, and organization perks. | Nexis's existing 55-achievement Legacy Points system already occupies this niche; the comparative takeaway is that spreading perk categories across *every* subsystem (not just combat) is what keeps the currency feeling relevant at all playstyles. | No new system proposed; recommend auditing Legacy Points spend categories for the same breadth (crafting, guild, marketplace, not just combat) rather than adding a second currency. | Don't introduce a second "merit-like" currency alongside Legacy Points — that fragments an already-working system. | low | yes |
| Torn (brief, comparative) | Player-run, multi-employee businesses ("Companies") are a distinct organization type from social/combat "Factions," with a director role, daily employee payouts, and business-specific stats like advertising and customer loyalty. | Validates Nexis's decision to keep Guilds (social/combat) and Consortiums (economic) as two separate organization types rather than merging them — Torn found enough value in the split to maintain three concepts (Faction/Company/city career) side by side. | No new mechanic; if Nexis ever adds player-run storefront businesses (see Kingdom of Loathing row below), keep them as individual player ventures distinct from Consortium treasuries, mirroring this separation. | Don't collapse Consortiums and any future individual-business feature into one entity — the genre precedent favors keeping them apart. | low | yes |
| Mafia Wars / Crime Empires / Crime Empire (mafia-crime browser genre) | Multiple resource bars (one for jobs, one for crimes/robbing, one for fighting) regenerate independently on separate timers, so each activity loop has its own cadence and doesn't cannibalize the others. | If any of Nexis's current bars are shared across unrelated activity loops (e.g., Adventures and Arena drawing from the same pool), splitting them the way this genre does would let players engage multiple loops per session instead of one crowding out the rest. | Introduce (or confirm already-separate) dedicated bars per major loop — e.g. a distinct "Resolve" bar for Adventures separate from an Arena "Vigor" bar — so upcoming item-gated adventure chains don't compete with Arena combat for the same resource. | Don't add a bar just to add one — only split pools where two genuinely different activities are currently forced to share one, and don't let the number of bars balloon past what a dense home dashboard can display cleanly. | medium | yes |
| Mafia Wars / Crime Empires / Cartel Empire | Player-owned businesses generate currency passively on fixed multi-hour timers and require a manual "collect" action; better business tiers are purchased as capital grows. | Gives high-capital players an individual (non-guild) income sink and a reason to log back in on a cadence, complementing rather than duplicating Consortium treasuries. | Add "Ventures" — individually owned small business slots (an apothecary stall, a forge, a caravan stop, etc., using original Nexis flavor) that accrue currency on a collection timer and can be upgraded tier by tier with player capital. | Don't make it fully automatic/idle — require the active collection click and periodic reinvestment decisions that the source genre uses, and don't let Ventures out-earn active play loops (Adventures, Arena, Jobs). | medium | yes |
| Mafia Wars / Crime Empires | Jobs/crimes are organized into rank tiers (low-tier to high-tier), each requiring specific gear or a rank threshold to attempt, with success chance and risk (arrest/injury) both increasing at higher tiers. | Directly validates the direction Nexis is already heading with item-gated adventure chaining, and suggests adding a success/fail branch with escalating risk at higher tiers rather than always-succeed encounters. | Structure the adventure chain as ranked "legs" with rising gear requirements and a chance of a setback (time lost, minor injury) at the hardest legs, distinct from a guaranteed pass/fail wall. | Don't copy exact tier names or numeric success-rate tables from any source game, and don't make failure so punishing that it blocks the only path through required story content — always keep a slower, certain path available. | high | yes |
| Crime Empires / Cartel Empire | A mid-size cooperative group ("Family"/"Cartel") sits between solo play and full diplomacy, mainly used for ranked group tournaments and internal leaderboards — a third structure alongside any faction/alliance layer those games have. | Mostly a validation note: Nexis's existing two-tier Guild (social/combat) and Consortium (economic) split already covers this space, so no third org type is needed — but the "periodic ranked group tournament" idea is a lightweight addition worth considering. | Add "Consortium Rivalries" — a periodic ranked leaderboard event scoring existing Consortiums against each other (e.g., aggregate weekly trade activity) without introducing any new organization type. | Don't add a third group tier (a "Family"/"Cartel" equivalent) — reuse the existing Guild/Consortium plumbing instead of forking it. | low | yes |
| Dark Throne | Character race/origin chosen at creation grants small ongoing passive trait differences (not full class rebalancing), alongside a persistent alliance system with shared messaging separate from combat. | Nexis's registration flow already has a class/portrait choice (Warrior, Mage, Rogue, Healer, Scout, Scholar per the current onboarding flow); attaching a minor passive perk to that choice would make an already-existing decision feel more meaningful without a rebalance. | Add small "Calling" perks — a minor passive bonus (a sliver of bonus XP in one subsystem, a small discount somewhere) tied to the class/portrait already chosen at character creation. | Keep the bonus flavor-level only; don't let starting class choice create a real power gap in combat or economy, and don't reuse any source game's race names. | medium | yes |
| Kingdom of Loathing | Certain adventure zones are locked behind a specific item (sometimes consumed on use, sometimes just carried/equipped) that must be found, crafted, or purchased before the next zone opens, which also creates market demand for those gating items. | This is the closest existing precedent for Nexis's planned item-gated adventure chaining (Adventure A drops an item Adventure B requires) — it shows the pattern works at scale and that making the gating item tradeable (not soulbound) turns the gate into a marketplace opportunity, not just a grind wall. | Call the gating drop class "Keystones" — items that unlock the next adventure in a chain, obtainable either as a drop from the prerequisite adventure or via purchase/trade on the marketplace, so unlucky players still have a path forward. | Don't make gating items soulbound with only one acquisition method — that removes the market-activity upside and can hard-stall players with bad luck; don't gate mainline story content so hard that there's no purchasable/craftable fallback. | high | yes |
| Kingdom of Loathing | Once a level threshold is hit, players can rent a personal storefront to list items at self-set prices, creating a real player-driven price economy independent of any NPC vendor. | Nexis's marketplace could grow beyond a single global exchange board by giving established players a personal storefront identity, encouraging active trading rather than passive listing. | Add "Nexis Stalls" — unlockable personal storefronts on the City Board, available once a level/reputation threshold is reached, where a player sets their own prices for listed items. | Avoid any loop that lets premium/real-money currency be laundered into freely tradeable in-game wealth through storefronts — keep premium currency non-resellable if Nexis has one; this exact failure mode is a known historical problem in games with unrestricted player shops. | medium | yes |
| Kingdom of Loathing | Guild-equivalent groups have a shared hall that members furnish using treasury funds — furniture grants small daily communal buffs (bonus currency/actions to each member), stat training tools, and unlocks harder cooperative content once enough has been invested. | Nexis Guilds already have backend-persisted treasuries; a hall-upgrade sink gives treasury size a visible, active purpose beyond a static number and gives guilds a shared long-term goal. | Add a "Guild Sanctum" upgrade track — treasury-funded room upgrades that grant small passive member-wide buffs (a sliver of bonus adventure yield or XP) and eventually unlock guild-only cooperative dungeon content. | Don't let hall buffs create a combat/economy gap so large that small or new guilds can't meaningfully compete elsewhere — cap the buff magnitude. | medium | yes |
| Kingdom of Loathing | Each adventure resolves as a full illustrated text vignette (a distinct page with its own art/flavor) before returning the player to the compact hub list of available adventures. | Reinforces that Nexis's adventure encounters benefit from a distinct "moment" page rather than resolving instantly inline — useful precedent for how the upcoming item-gated adventure chain should present each leg. | Give each adventure resolution its own full-panel result page (own layout, own art slot, own reveal of loot/Keystone drops) before returning to the compact adventure list, rather than showing results as an inline toast. | Don't copy any specific illustration style, joke format, or flavor text — design original Nexis art direction and voice for these result pages. | medium | yes |
| Fallen London / StoryNexus (quality-based narrative engine) | Encounters ("storylets") are drawn from a pool gated by numeric player "qualities" (arbitrary tracked stats/flags, not just items); branch options can be hidden, shown, or locked depending on those quality values, generalizing "gating" beyond physical items. | Generalizes the item-gate concept Nexis is about to build: not every prerequisite needs to be a physical item — some adventure chain links could gate on an earned flag/reputation value instead, enabling narrative-only unlocks without new item art. | Add a lightweight "Deeds" flag system that runs alongside the item-gate system — completing Adventure A can set an internal flag that gates Adventure C even with no physical item involved, useful for pure story beats. | Don't build a full generic rules/quality engine to replace the item-gate system — add Deeds as a narrow, additive extension, and don't adopt any of the source's Victorian-gothic setting or terminology. | medium | yes |
| Legend of the Green Dragon | Free-roam combat encounters are capped per game-day and offer a risk selector (fight something weaker for a smaller, safer reward or something tougher for a bonus); a clean win can grant an extra free turn, letting skilled play chain into a streak. | Gives players agency over risk/reward on existing Arena or Adventure encounters without requiring new content — reusing existing encounters at adjustable difficulty is cheap to build and adds replay value. | Add a "Bold Strike" difficulty toggle before an Arena or Adventure encounter (Cautious/Standard/Reckless), scaling reward and setback risk accordingly, with consecutive clean wins building a small "Momentum" bonus. | Don't reuse the source's exact terminology, and keep this separate from any future crime/adventure-chain resource cost so the two systems don't confusingly stack bar costs. | medium | yes |
| Urban Dead | All actions (move, fight, search, fortify) draw from one shared, slowly-regenerating action-point pool instead of several separate systems, and there are no NPCs at all — every encounter is player vs. player in a shared physical map of locations. | Mostly a cautionary contrast rather than something to adopt wholesale, since Nexis's NPC-driven Arena/Adventures are core to its identity — the one narrow transferable idea is a lightweight "who else is here" presence signal in a shared location. | Add a small "Nearby Wanderers" counter showing how many other online players are currently in the same City Board zone or dungeon tier — a presence signal only, not a shared-pool or base-building system. | Don't merge Nexis's distinct resource bars into one universal pool, and don't remove NPC-driven content chasing a fully emergent, player-only design — that would abandon Nexis's Arena/Adventure identity. | low | yes |
| Cyber Nations (filtered — nation-management genre, mostly out of scope) | Alliances (mostly coordinated off-site) let member nations share technology/resources with each other on top of each nation's own daily tax-collection cycle. | Nexis is explicitly not building a 4X nation-management layer, so almost none of this transfers; the one narrow, bounded idea is a small inter-Consortium resource-sharing agreement, kept intentionally minor. | Add "Consortium Trade Pacts" — a capped, opt-in resource-gift or discount agreement between two allied Consortiums, explicitly small in scope and not a stepping stone toward nation simulation. | Do not build any land/infrastructure/tax nation-simulation layer, resource-type roster, or daily collect-and-reinvest economy at the organization level — this is the core genre Nexis has decided not to become. | low | yes |
| NationStates (filtered — mostly out of scope) | Nations periodically receive multiple-choice narrative "issue" events that nudge a few nation-wide stats, with no combat or trading involved in the core loop. | Very weak fit for a Torn-style RPG, but the narrow "occasional narrative decision popup with a small, bounded stat consequence" presentation pattern could add flavor to Guild/Consortium leadership without any deep new system. | Add rare optional "Council Dispatches" — short text decision prompts for guild/consortium leaders with small, capped consequences (minor treasury or morale shift), purely for flavor. | Don't attempt any version of the source's government-classification or ideology-tracking system — that entire genre (issues-only, no combat/trading) is not the game Nexis is building. | low | yes |
| Melvor Idle (filtered — idle-RPG genre, only narrowly relevant) | Skills are deliberately interdependent (gathering feeds crafting feeds combat gear), and a secondary "mastery" experience track layered on top of the main skill level rewards repeating the same specific action over time; the game also simulates offline/AFK progression. | The skill-interdependency chain is a useful sanity check for Nexis's Education/Jobs/Crafting relationships (each should feed the next), and a secondary per-action mastery track is a repeatable long-tail reward that doesn't require idle/offline simulation to work. | Add a "Refinement" sub-track — a slow secondary XP bar tied to one specific, frequently repeated Adventure or crafting recipe, granting small quality-of-life unlocks (slightly faster completion, slightly better yield) the more that exact action is repeated. | Do not add offline/AFK progression simulation or any other idle-game mechanic — Nexis has explicitly ruled out becoming an idle game, so only the interdependency and mastery-track ideas should be taken, not the offline simulation. | low | yes |
| Cross-game moderation/trust-and-safety pattern (industry-wide, not one specific title) | Mature live-service moderation setups use a graduated sanction ladder (warning, temporary restriction, ranked/feature lockout, permanent ban), a player-facing report intake queue, and a built-in appeals flow — treating individual player reports as noisy signal requiring corroboration rather than automatic action. | Nexis's existing admin panel is already server-authoritative with full audit logging of every admin action, but per current docs it centers on direct one-off stat/inventory corrections rather than a graduated sanction ladder or a player-facing report/appeal flow. | Add a "Warden's Ledger" sanction ladder (flagged warning, timed travel/jail lock, full suspension) plus a simple player report intake queue and a player-visible appeal request, all tied into the existing audit log rather than replacing it. | Don't let automated systems act on unverified player reports alone — keep a human admin decision in the loop, consistent with Nexis's current server-authoritative, no-bulk-tools admin posture. | medium | yes |
| Mafia Wars and similar Facebook-era crime-genre dashboards | The home screen itself is dense but compact: resource bars up top, one-click quick-action buttons for the core loops, and a small activity feed (friends'/faction members' recent actions) embedded directly on the dashboard rather than requiring navigation away. | Nexis already favors a dense, Torn-style home dashboard; the "quick actions plus a compact activity feed live on the home page" pattern is a proven way to surface guild/consortium and social activity without adding a new page. | Add a compact "Chronicle Feed" module to the existing home dashboard — a small scrolling list of recent guild/consortium member activity and personal milestones, sitting alongside existing bars and quick-action buttons. | Don't let the feed grow into a full social-network timeline or push the dashboard past a single, no-scroll compact view — keep it to a handful of recent lines. | medium | yes |
| Mafia Wars and similar crime-genre browser RPGs | Completing a job/crime resolves as a distinct full-screen "mission complete" card with narrative flavor text and a loot/XP/cash reveal, separate from the compact job-list hub. | Gives Nexis a template for how Job or Crime-chain resolutions (not just Adventures) could get their own visual beat, reinforcing consistency with the Kingdom of Loathing adventure-vignette pattern above. | Reuse the same "Mission Chronicle" full-panel result page pattern (proposed above for Adventures) for Job and future Crime-chain resolutions too, so all major activity loops share one consistent interstitial result format. | Don't invent a third, different interstitial format for Jobs versus Adventures versus Crimes — consolidate on one shared result-page component to avoid UI fragmentation. | low | yes |

## Highest-confidence recommendation

The strongest, most directly actionable finding is the **Kingdom of Loathing
item-gated zone progression pattern** (row: "Kingdom of Loathing — item-gated
zone progression"). It validates the exact direction Nexis is already heading
with item-gated adventure chaining, and adds one concrete refinement worth
carrying forward: make the gating item ("Keystone") tradeable/purchasable on the
marketplace rather than soulbound-only, so a player who doesn't get a lucky drop
from Adventure A still has a path to Adventure B through trade or a slower
alternate grind. This keeps the chain from becoming a hard progression wall while
still creating organic marketplace demand for the gating items.
